# Melody corpus candidates v0.1

This is a planning list for Museeka Melody Lab. It deliberately separates **composition candidates we may be able to commit as canonical note data** from **reference-only works whose note sequences should not be redistributed by the public repository without a suitable licence or a clear public-domain basis**.

Legal status is jurisdiction-dependent. Every committed canonical melody still needs source/provenance review. For public-domain compositions, prefer our own transcription from a public-domain score/source; a modern arrangement or MIDI transcription can have separate rights.

## Status vocabulary

- `PD-TARGET`: old/traditional composition intended for the committed corpus after source/jurisdiction verification.
- `REFERENCE-ONLY`: keep metadata/title in the public repo; note data enters only through user-supplied/local or licensed material unless rights are cleared.

## Selection principles for the eventual training/evaluation corpus

The final corpus should not simply maximize recognizability. It should deliberately span:

- high vs low repeated-note density;
- stepwise vs leaping contours;
- regular vs asymmetric rhythm;
- exact motif repetition vs continuous variation;
- narrow vs wide pitch range;
- strong vs weak phrase closure;
- major/minor/modal/ambiguous tonal language;
- monophonic song melodies vs instrumental themes;
- short hooks vs long lyrical phrases;
- familiar and unfamiliar material;
- Western classical/pop plus traditional melodies from multiple musical cultures.

For preference experiments, familiarity must remain a separate listener annotation rather than being treated as a property of the melody itself.
